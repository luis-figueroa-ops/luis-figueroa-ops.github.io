I2B Channel Buildout

Verizon Business's indirect retail channel ran on manual tick-mark accounting with no formal infrastructure. I built the systems, governance, and AI-enabled workflows that scaled it into a fully operational channel reaching 8,000+ external agents, then owned the day-to-day training, issue resolution, and budget decisions that kept it running.

Verizon Business

At Verizon Business, I played a central role in the sales operations and digital transformation of a newly formalized indirect SMB retail channel, aligning technology, AI initiatives, and scalable processes with field execution. This is that story.

Formalizing the Channel's Operational Infrastructure

Before it became part of Verizon Business, the Authorized Retailer Business channel operated under Verizon Consumer, where partners tracked their own sales and expected payouts through manual, sometimes literal tick-mark systems. The channel was producing results, but it had no formal infrastructure behind it, no integration with Verizon Business's systems, reporting, or support ecosystem.

When Verizon leadership decided to formally launch it as an official SMB sales channel, our Sales Operations team reorganized into dedicated groups, and I was responsible for building the operational foundation from the ground up. The work sat inside a matrixed structure that made it harder than it sounds: Verizon Consumer owned the partner contracts and source data, while Verizon Business owned execution, and the two had never operated on the same systems. Consumer didn't run on Salesforce at all, which meant none of the partner data Verizon Business needed to manage, track, or scale the channel actually lived where the rest of the business worked.

I unified those pieces by integrating partner sales activity directly into Salesforce, establishing commission plans and performance targets, and building the reporting and audit workflows needed for accurate account alignment, quota management, and revenue tracking. I also partnered with stakeholders to bring in tools like Artemis List Builder for prospecting and later Velocity AI, giving partner agents the same targeted insights internal reps had access to.

The result was a channel that ran on the same systems, governance, and support ecosystem as the rest of Verizon Business — service, chat, bots, order management, financial operations — instead of manual workarounds. Partners no longer needed account managers as go-betweens for basic support, which freed those account managers to focus on strategic growth instead of playing intermediary.

Driving AI Adoption Across the Channel

Being selected as a High-Potential employee for Verizon's AI Automation and Adoption Pillar meant exploring what AI could realistically do for the business, not just what was possible in theory. Part of that work involved benchmarking AI capabilities across top tech companies to generate ideas for Verizon's internal AI roadmap, then figuring out which of those ideas could actually move the needle inside sales operations.

Working with IT, I helped expand Salesforce's Velocity AI so it could interpret billing data and surface upsell opportunities and customer trends reps would otherwise have had to dig for manually. It also gave reps a single place to see consolidated customer insights, business profile data, web research, reviews, industry context, and let them run mock customer Q&A and pushback simulations to prepare for objections before a call instead of during one. I also helped shape AI-driven email prioritization, so reps could surface urgent, business-critical messages instead of digging through a flooded inbox.

On the enablement side, I trained Sr. Managers on integrating Gemini GEM and NotebookLM into live project workflows, showing them how to automate meeting summaries and generate concise All-Hands recaps for field teams. I also converted my 70+ page "Where Do I Go?" sales playbook into an AI-assisted workflow, so reps could query guidance directly instead of manually searching through decks and documents.

Key Results
  • Partnered with IT on enterprise AI initiative, converting a 70-page Indirect channel playbook into an AI-queryable workflow and defining field-ready use cases, briefing AVP leadership for 90+ account managers.

Scaling Enablement to 8,000+ Agents

New system launches only work if the people using them actually understand what changed and why. For B360 POS and Salesforce's Artemis List Builder, I partnered directly with the product grooming and design teams during development, representing the indirect channel's perspective and validating proposed functionality against how the field actually worked. That meant advocating for accurate Jira user story requirements so releases didn't ship with gaps that conflicted with what the channel needed, and verifying training materials before launch so frontline sellers had what they needed from day one.

When post-launch challenges surfaced anyway — user confusion, underutilized features — I built playbooks and simplified reference guides to close those gaps. Those materials ended up getting used well beyond the original training sessions. The 70+ slide "Where Do I Go?" guide I built independently, centralizing process ownership, escalation paths, and troubleshooting steps, later got rebuilt inside Salesforce with Velocity AI, turning a static reference document into interactive, in-platform guidance reps could query directly.

Key Results
  • Led a cross-functional channel initiative to enable a Salesforce prospecting tool to 8,000+ Indirect partner sellers, resolving the legal and compliance barriers to drive a 20% gain in sales productivity.
  • Coordinated POS testing calls with design teams and front-line sellers across the 8,000+ Indirect agent partner channel, surfacing field feedback to refine the new system ahead of legacy sunset.

Owning Issue Resolution

A lot of operational friction never shows up as one big problem — it shows up as a steady stream of smaller ones that all trace back to the same root cause. I served as the point of contact and SME between field reps and internal stakeholders for system, credit, and porting issues creating barriers to deal closure and hurting the customer experience.

Rather than resolving each escalation individually and moving on, I looked for the patterns underneath them, forecasting emerging issues from what I was seeing repeat across cross-team calls, and working with stakeholders to build permanent fixes and stronger escalation paths instead of one-off patches.

Key Results
  • Identified root causes behind recurring escalations by building a Slack workflow to analyze data, create a standardized playbook, and work cross-functionally, reducing volume 50% (100+/month) over 8 months.

Closing System and Reporting Gaps

Not every fix required a full system rebuild — some just needed a specific gap closed. When a Salesforce lead-to-opportunity routing issue was forcing reps into a manual workaround, I drove the automation enhancement that eliminated it. Separately, I closed an outlet identification gap that had been creating commission delays and inaccurate new location assignments.

Key Results
  • Resolved a Salesforce lead-to-opportunity routing gap, driving an automation enhancement that eliminated a manual workaround and protected up to 40 leads per cycle from revenue leakage.
  • Eliminated 7-day commission lag and improved new location assignment accuracy by closing an outlet identification gap with a new reporting process, reducing quota discrepancies by 16%.

Realigning Four Organizations After a Reorg

A reorg hit mid-cycle and eliminated the team that had handled promo resolution for 8,000+ agent partners — no handoff, no replacement. What was left was 6,000+ monthly submissions with nowhere to go, and 90+ account managers fielding calls they had no way to answer.

I had four organizations that had never formally worked together: the agents themselves, a newly formed Center of Excellence, the Business Finance Operations center, and the Business and Government Customer Operations center. I applied structured change management across all of them simultaneously, building awareness of the new process, addressing resistance through hands-on enablement, and closing knowledge gaps through documented, step-by-step training.

I mapped the process at three levels of detail: a high-level overview for leadership, a functional ownership map showing exactly who owned each handoff, and rep-level documentation detailed enough to execute without assistance. Along the way, cross-functional stakeholder alignment surfaced a policy misalignment between two centers that had been generating incorrect denials — I resolved it by getting decision-makers to review the same evidence together instead of working from conflicting assumptions.

The result was a standardized intake and escalation process that held: 6,000+ monthly submissions routed correctly, unauthorized credits blocked through structured validation, and 90+ account managers protected from becoming the default escalation path. Submission volume actually declined month over month afterward — a signal that agents were getting it right the first time instead of relying on manual correction, proof the change had taken hold rather than just being tolerated.

Key Results
  • Aligned four cross-functional organizations to transition promo resolution work into a newly centralized Indirect COE team post-reorg, safeguarding 8,000+ agent partner sellers and 6,000+ monthly submissions.

Data-Driven Budget and Program Governance

A $5M/year equipment discount budget, designed to accelerate deal closure for high-risk or hesitant customers, was being overutilized due to inconsistent tracking and limited accountability. Before the program even launched, I flagged the risk: its initial complexity was going to cause backend processing delays for Finance and Offline Support, rep confusion, and a degraded customer experience, so I built simplified policies and documentation upfront to head that off.

When leadership needed to evaluate the program's profitability and effectiveness after years of investment, I redesigned it around a Smartsheet workflow with governance controls and a performance dashboard built for ongoing automation, replacing the inconsistent tracking that had let overspend go unchecked. That visibility also clarified complex eligibility rules tied to product mix and transaction type — new lines, upgrades, ports — that had been a source of confusion.

Key Results
  • Redesigned a $5M annual budget program by implementing a Smartsheet workflow, adding governance controls and a performance dashboard, reducing channel spending by $750K, and enabling automation.

Separately, when a new bot-driven automation inside B360 POS launched to reduce service transactions and offline submission requests, usage data showed the field wasn't leveraging it — reps still weren't resolving eligible discount and feature code changes for bundled offers directly in the system. I built a detailed field playbook with real-world scenarios and troubleshooting examples so reps could recognize exactly when to use the automated function instead of falling back on manual workarounds. Adoption improved, transactions got faster and more independent, and backend teams were freed up for higher-value work.
